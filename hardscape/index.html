Why so many paver patios and retaining walls are built to sell instead of built to last

Too much hardscaping around here is built to sell. A crew lays it fast, collects the check and moves on to the next yard. When a patio or wall isn't built to last:

How Amanda's Villa builds a paver patio that stays level

Ninety percent of a patio is underground, so that's where the work goes.

Built from the gravel up

Colton's crew digs out eight to ten inches, packs in a gravel subbase, sets a finer base on top and locks the joints with polymeric sand. Water drains through. Weeds stay out. The patio stays level.

One yard at a time

Once the crew starts a job, it stays on that job until it's finished. Nobody comes home to a half-dug patio for a month while the crew works across town.

Designed with the whole yard

Colton builds the stone and Amanda plans the planting around it, so the patio, the walls and the beds look like they belong together.

What does paver patio installation look like on day one?

Colton warns every customer before the first shovel goes in: "Look, it looks way worse before it gets better." The crew digs ten to twelve inches down, and for a day or two your yard looks like someone's building a pool in it.

Paver patio and retaining wall questions

Is the estimate free?

Yes. Colton comes out, surveys the yard with a transit and gives an estimate at no charge.

Why does a patio need so much gravel underneath?

About 90 percent of a patio is underground. Eight to ten inches of packed gravel lets water drain through and keeps the pavers from sinking or shifting. Pavers laid on plain sand tend to move after the first heavy rain.

What is polymeric sand?

It's the sand swept into the joints between pavers. Once it's wet, it sets firm, a bit like mortar, which holds the pavers in place and keeps weeds out.

Will the crew leave my job half finished?

No. Once the crew starts a job, it stays on it until it's done. Weather is the only thing that holds a job up, mostly spring storms.

Can you work with my budget?

Yes. Colton designs the job he'd want for his own yard, then scales the design to fit your budget. Most projects start around $1,500. Large hillside jobs can run past $100,000.

Do you build retaining walls on steep lots?

Yes. The crew builds tall structural walls on hillside lots, including a wall in Hot Springs holding back more than 80 tons of gravel.

Can you rebuild a wall someone else built?

Yes. Colton can look at an existing wall, tell you whether it's sound, and rebuild it if it isn't.

Do you install French drains?

Yes. Not every yard needs one, but when water sits in a yard, a French drain usually fixes it.

Can Amanda do the planting around the patio?

Yes. Amanda designs the planting and Colton builds the stone, so the two are planned together.
