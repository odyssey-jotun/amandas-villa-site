Why standing water collects in Arkansas yards

Arkansas averages about 50 inches of rain a year, and heavy storms can drop several inches in a single day.1 Much of the state's soil drains slowly, so when that rain has nowhere to go:

Water sits where it falls

Low spots stay soggy for days and runoff collects against the foundation. Roots in saturated soil run short of oxygen,2 and standing water can breed grass-killing diseases.3

Every storm becomes a worry

Homeowners end up watching the forecast, laying boards across the mud and wondering what all that water is doing to the house.

A yard should dry out after it rains

No family should have to wait a week after every storm before the kids can play in the backyard again.

French drain questions

What is a French drain?

A French drain is a gravel-filled trench, usually with a perforated pipe along the bottom, that collects water from the soil and the surface and carries it downhill to a spot where it can drain away. It is named for Henry Flagg French, a New Hampshire judge and farmer who described the method in his 1859 book Farm Drainage.4

How does a French drain work?

Water moves through loose gravel far faster than through dense, saturated soil. It seeps into the gravel, flows along the pipe by gravity and leaves the yard at the outlet, which is why every drain is laid to a surveyed slope.

How deep should a French drain be?

It depends on the water it needs to catch. A typical residential trench runs 18 to 24 inches deep and 12 to 18 inches wide.5 The team sets the depth, route and slope of your drain from the transit survey.

How much slope does a French drain need?

The trench needs a fall of at least 1 percent, about one inch for every eight feet.6 The transit survey shows whether your yard has that fall naturally or whether the trench needs to create it.

What does the filter fabric do?

Filter fabric wraps the gravel and keeps the surrounding soil from washing in. Soil mixed into the gravel clogs the open spaces the water flows through.5

Does a French drain need an outlet?

Yes. The water has to leave the trench somewhere lower than where it entered, such as a lower part of the property. The outlet is chosen during the site survey, before any digging starts.

Can a French drain run along the foundation?

Yes. A drain beside the house catches water before it collects against the foundation. It works best with ground that also slopes away from the house, and Arkansas building code calls for a fall of at least six inches within the first ten feet.7

Will buried utility lines be marked before digging?

Arkansas law requires notice to Arkansas 811 at least two full working days before digging, so buried utility lines are located and marked before a trench is opened.8
